### 0.4 The conclusion the numbers force

The binding constraint on this system making money is **not** strategy quality,
model quality, or exit timing. It is that **almost nothing is live, the one live
thing is $280, and several declared-live legs cannot place an order at all**.

An enormous, genuinely rigorous research apparatus (M20–M36) is producing

backlog carries 180 open items, of which several are *"this declared-live leg
places nothing"*.

**Therefore the next work is capability integrity, not more research.** Research
lanes continue, but behind it.

#### Two items moved to M20 (recorded so the handoff is not re-derived)

**0.1 — the IB fix is M20's (#9240), not this branch's.** This plan originally
carried a lock serialising `IBMarketData` against the order path. That was
**insufficient, and M20's review said so correctly**: a lock serialises but does
not *pin*, so thread B can still drive the event loop thread A created — and
since `ib_insync` binds the socket transport to one loop, driving it from another
thread is the documented hang. Mutual exclusion made the two threads take turns
at the unsafe thing. Their single-worker executor + `_on_ib_fetch_thread()` pin
closes it. Mine was dropped wholesale, along with its test (which asserted
`_ib_lock` — an identifier that no longer exists, so it would have *failed*, not
merely gone stale).

**0.4 — the venue-max clamp is M20's too.** They root-caused it:
`risk.py::position_size` resolves `qty_step` and `min_qty` and caps by margin,
but carries **no `max_qty` clamp anywhere**, so the margin pre-flight cap is free
to exceed the venue ceiling and nothing catches it before submit. That is
Tier-3 (sizing) and **not AVAX-specific** — any symbol whose venue `max_qty` sits
below `available × leverage × buffer / price` has the same shape, real money
included. The separation measurement below still stands as independent
confirmation, and it agrees.

#### The standing check — `scripts/ops/dead_leg_audit.py` (built 2026-08-14)

Neither existing check can see this class. `/health-review`'s **strategy
silence** check measures `*_eval` events — a leg that evaluates, signals, and has
every order bounced is *not silent*, it is loudly failing at the last step, and
grades `ok`. `account_reachability_alert` probes `positions()` — the account
answers. So "declared live, signals, places NOTHING" falls between them.

**First run, 14d window over the trainer's synced journal (trainer-diag #9262):
72 legs graded, 32 `signalled_never_placed`.** The verdict is descriptive, not a
defect claim — the refusal *reason* is what separates a broken leg from a correct
refusal, and both are present:

**⚠️ TWO RULES FOR READING THIS OUTPUT, both learned by getting it wrong first:**

1. **`MAX(entry_reason)` is a SAMPLE, not the population.** I reported
   `ib_paper`/`mgc_trend_1h` as "43 rejections, ALL `balance() returned None`"
   on the strength of one sampled reason string. Counting the actual predicate
   gives **5**, not 43 — the other ~38 are
   `intent_noop:flip_suppressed_hold_policy`, which is the hold policy working
   *correctly* on the held 105-contract MGC position. **Always count the
   predicate before quoting a cause.**
2. **A window that straddles a fix makes a fixed bug look current.** Read the

**The venue-max finding is confirmed by perfect separation.** Every
`ict_scalp_avax_5m` order over 14d, against Bybit's 22,000 AVAXUSDT cap:

- `exchange_rejected`: **n=18, range 24,357.8 – 34,526.3 — all ABOVE the cap**

#### The `alpaca_live` `zero_balance` row — challenged, and upheld

Worth writing down because the challenge *failed*, and a plan that only records
its confirmations is not honest about how it was built.

Reading `coordinator.py` for Lane 0.3 turned up that `_default_balance_fetcher`'s
branch 3 returns **`0.0`** on a whole-fetch exception, which surfaces as exactly
the `zero_balance: gate_balance=0.00` string above. That made the "correct — a
$0.10 account" reading look like it might be a misattribution: the same symptom
has two possible causes, and one of them is a bug.

So it was measured rather than flipped (trainer-diag #9293), with a
**discriminator** chosen in advance: a balance-service outage refuses every
routed account inside one tick, so it must **burst** across several `account_id`s
within an hour; an unfunded account **trickles** on one account over many days.

The answer was unambiguous — 120 refusals, `alpaca_live` **alone**, 3–5 per hour
across 16 separate days, with exactly one hour in the whole window touching a
second account. That is the trickle. The original reading stands, the branch-3
hypothesis is **not** what produced these rows, and branch 3 is filed as *latent*
(`BL-20260814-BALANCE-FETCHER-BRANCH3-ZERO-AND-A-LOG-THAT-LIES`) rather than as an
active cause.

One thing the same pull *did* surface: `balance() returned None` is low-volume
(**6 in 30d**), but **4 of the 6 land on 2026-08-13 across three different
accounts** — which is the burst signature, on the branch that refuses loudly.
That belongs to `BL-20260813-ALPACA-BALANCE-NONE-WHILE-ACCOUNT-READS-ACTIVE`.

⚠️ **A third rule for reading this audit, learned the same way as the first two:
the journal you query must have a denominator.** The first three attempts at the
query above returned `(zero rows)` for every question — because the trainer's
canonical resolver points at an **empty** stray journal (0 rows, stale since
2026-08-02) while the real 4,649-row one sits under `data/`. Only a sanity probe
placed *first* stopped that from being reported as "no such refusals exist".
Filed as `BL-20260814-TRAINER-CANONICAL-RESOLVER-POINTS-AT-EMPTY-JOURNAL`, and
`dead_leg_audit.py` now refuses a zero-row journal outright rather than printing
an all-clear.
